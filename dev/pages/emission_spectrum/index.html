Conditional photon emission spectra

An intrinsic spectrum distributes already produced internal photons over wavelength. It is separate from leaf absorption, pigment/PSII allocation, physiological fluorescence yield, internal escape and canopy transport. The core supplies no default empirical spectrum.

# Inputs and their units come from the caller's explicit experiment parameters.
mass = photon_density_per_nm .* quadrature_width_nm
source = TabulatedPhotonSpectrum(wavelength_nm, mass)
probability = emission_probabilities(source, StrictStokes(), excitation_nm)

TabulatedPhotonSpectrum stores a discrete photon measure, not a density. Multiply by the spectral integration measure once before construction. Wavelengths and masses must share a floating-point type; Float32 and Float64 are tested. Inputs are copied, but stored vectors remain mutable; preparation revalidates them. Host preparation retains raw relative masses and filters support before max-scaled normalization, so an extremely small retained tail survives even when excluded values are large. A retained mass smaller than the floating-point range relative to other retained masses can still round to zero; this is not arbitrary precision. Probabilities stay on the original grid with exact excluded zeros.

StrictStokes() permits only emission wavelengths longer than excitation. It is an explicit approximation excluding thermal anti-Stokes excitation. Conditioning changes the reference shape while preserving the separately prescribed photon yield. Removed reference weights do not establish extra heat or quenching. Empty positive support is an error. A physically dark source should be skipped by the caller rather than requesting an undefined conditional distribution.

Combining with optical escape

For excitation node e and emission node m, prepare photon probabilities p[m,e]. Evaluate the independent optical fates at each emitted wavelength and the appropriate excitation-dependent birth profile. The front, back and reabsorbed operators are

\[M_{F,m e}=p_{m e}F_{m e},\quad M_{B,m e}=p_{m e}B_{m e},\quad M_{A,m e}=p_{m e}A_{m e}.\]

Each excitation column sums over wavelengths and fates to one, within roundoff. Multiply these operators by already-produced photons in each excitation bin. Do not multiply absorption or yield again. Share operators across equivalent optical/source classes instead of storing one spectral tensor per leaf population. The matrices can be transferred to CPU/GPU backends for batched application; the vector-owning source object and validation stay on the host.

Convert all three photon outcomes to energy using their emission wavelengths. Strict Stokes support gives a nonnegative excitation-to-emission energy difference per photon. This source/escape accounting is not a complete biochemical or thermal budget. Reabsorption terminates the photon; secondary fluorescence is separate. The regular-plate fates are hemispheric face totals. Directional canopy SIF needs its own face-radiance closure and canopy transport.

Replacing a component

Subtype AbstractEmissionSupport and implement the scalar Boolean emission_allowed(policy, emission_nm, excitation_nm) for a different support rule. Inputs provided by preparation are finite positive wavelengths of the same type. The tests demonstrate a finite Stokes band without changing the source, escape or physiology code. A future analytic or state-dependent spectrum can dispatch on the first argument of emission_probabilities; no storage registry or canopy-grid dependency is required.

Scalar validation compares normalization with BigFloat, including support at equality, rare tails, unequal caller-owned measures, mutable inputs and invalid precision. Prepared CPU/CUDA matrix tests cover both precisions, dark columns, multiple excitation distributions, front/rear birth profiles, reordered and empty batches, photon closure and emitted-energy bounds. These are numerical and interface checks, not empirical calibration of a spectral shape.

API reference: TabulatedPhotonSpectrum, AbstractEmissionSupport, StrictStokes, emission_allowed, and emission_probabilities.
