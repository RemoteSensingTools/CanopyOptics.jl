Internal leaf photon escape

RegularPlateEscape computes the fates of a photon already produced inside one symmetric regular leaf plate. It returns front escape, back escape and terminal material reabsorption. The birth-depth model is a separate argument:

escape = RegularPlateEscape(emission_depth, interface_transmission)
birth = ExponentialBirthProfile(excitation_depth, front_amplitude, rear_amplitude)
fates = leaf_emission_fates(escape, birth)
uniform_fates = leaf_emission_fates(escape, UniformBirthProfile())

All numerical inputs use the same Float32 or Float64. Construct validated parameters on the host, then apply the scalar function or broadcast it over arrays of prepared parameters on CPU/CUDA. Both built-in birth choices are isbits objects; application allocates no host memory. The module is src/leaf_optics/plate_emission.jl.

What the parameters mean

Depth z=0 is the fixed anatomical front and z=1 the back. Effective excitation depth a and emission depth b are nonnegative dimensionless attenuation depths. They describe traversal survival exp(-a) and exp(-b). Interface transmission t is the internal-to-outside probability at each boundary, distinct from the traversal survival. Reflection is r=1-t. Require 0<t≤1; a perfectly trapping lossless plate needs a fourth, retained-photon fate and is excluded.

The exponential birth density is proportional to

\[f\exp(-az)+g\exp[-a(1-z)].\]

The host constructor normalizes nonnegative relative amplitudes with max scaling; both normalized components are retained when representable. An all-zero pair is an error because it cannot define a conditional birth density. Zero excitation depth gives the uniform profile even when the amplitudes differ. It does not assert that a leaf with no absorbed light produces fluorescence.

For front illumination of a homogeneous regular plate, relative amplitudes are (1, r_exc*exp(-a)). The excitation depth uses total material attenuation. Homogeneous pigment allocation and depth-independent physiological yield cancel when conditioning on an internal birth. This effective exponential internal-depth closure is an approximation, as in Platespect, not an exact angular solution of light inside a leaf. This component does not invert fractional PROSPECT stacks into possibly nonpassive effective interfaces.

Photon paths and numerical limits

Emission initially travels toward either face with probability one half. Let F₀,B₀ be first-arrival probabilities after within-plate absorption. Reflected traversals have survival q=r*exp(-b), giving

\[F=t(F_0+qB_0)/(1-q^2),\qquad B=t(B_0+qF_0)/(1-q^2).\]

Implementation factors the denominator using the retained transmission and direct traversal loss -expm1(-b). Reabsorption is computed as first-flight loss plus absorption during subsequent reflected traversals. It is not reconstructed solely as a small residual of rounded escaped probabilities. No result is clipped or rescaled. Lossless emission gives (1/2,1/2,0) for any supported birth profile. The initial reabsorption derivative is (1+r)/(2t), independently of birth depth.

Normalized exponential overlap integrals handle equal depths, finite depths whose sum overflows and sharply localized sources. Below b=eps(FT)^(1/4), a four-term moment expansion preserves weak absorption. Positive moment series and a large-depth recurrence avoid cancellation at small excitation depth. These thresholds and coefficients are numerical algorithm choices, not physical constants. Relative reabsorption accuracy near the Taylor/direct-integral switch need not equal machine epsilon; the independent test envelope is 3e-5 in Float32 and 2e-11 in Float64, with a few-subnormal-ULP absolute allowance in batch checks.

Tests include independent 2048-bit boundary balances, 90-digit numerical source depth integration with explicit repeated-reflection paths, numerical switch neighborhoods, face reversal, zero/weak/opaque absorption, normalized extreme amplitudes and smallest-positive interface transmission. CPU and CUDA batches exercise both birth choices, reordered and empty arrays. ForwardDiff is not yet a supported contract for these preparation types.

The validation record includes source and reference hashes, test logs and 1,230 scalar accuracy probes per precision. Against references rounded to the tested FT, the measured maxima were:

Composition and extension

Neither the escape model nor the birth profile owns an intrinsic emission spectrum, absorptance, pigment selection, PSII allocation or physiological yield. Those quantities enter the surrounding calculation once. A spectral operator must supply explicit emission measures and wavelength support; emission energy uses emission wavelength. Photon conservation here is not a complete leaf energy budget. Reabsorbed photons terminate; secondary fluorescence is a separate model.

These are hemispheric face totals. Angular emission into a canopy requires a separate face-radiance closure. Two-sided excitation retains distinct source contributions; anatomical face labels do not change with illumination. A custom birth type can subtype AbstractLeafBirthProfile and implement leaf_emission_fates(escape, birth); a different escape structure can dispatch on the first argument. Neither extension changes a photosynthesis model or host grid.

The separate emission spectrum interface distributes already-produced photons over wavelength with an explicit support rule. Multiple-plate assembly and canopy coupling follow separately. No calibrated intrinsic PSII shape, fractional-stack, directional SIF or complete coupled energy capability is implied by this scalar component.

Preparing a regular plate from material properties

diffuse_plate_optics prepares effective depths and interface probabilities from dimensionless material optical depth k and refractive index nr on the host. Its separate implementation is src/leaf_optics/plate_preparation.jl. Material composition and optical-table selection remain caller choices.

excitation = diffuse_plate_optics(excitation_material_depth, excitation_index)
emission = diffuse_plate_optics(emission_material_depth, emission_index)
birth = ExponentialBirthProfile(excitation.effective_depth,
    one(excitation_material_depth),
    (one(excitation_material_depth)-excitation.interface_exit_transmission) *
        excitation.traversal_transmission)
escape = RegularPlateEscape(emission.effective_depth,
    emission.interface_exit_transmission)
fates = leaf_emission_fates(escape, birth)

Traversal transmission is 2E₃(k). The independently evaluated traversal loss preserves weak absorption even when transmission rounds to one. Effective depth uses -log1p(-loss) near the conservative limit and -log(transmission) otherwise. A loss rounded to one is allowed when transmission remains positive. If the E₃ library underflows to zero, preparation rejects the unresolved depth; it supplies no optical floor. Independently resolved finite depths can still be passed directly to the scalar component.

Both interface probabilities use hemispheric 90-degree Fresnel transmission; internal-to-outside transmission equals outside-to-inside transmission divided by nr². This differs from default PROSPECT's 40-degree first-entry cone. Under this isotropized interior closure, the entry cone changes source amplitude but not the normalized birth shape. It does not resolve angle-dependent internal paths. For a unit incident photon, the internal absorption source is

\[B=\frac{t_{\mathrm{entry}}\,a_{\mathrm{traversal}}} {t_{\mathrm{exit}}+(1-t_{\mathrm{exit}})a_{\mathrm{traversal}}}.\]

Use a matching entry convention when comparing this source with an external absorption budget. Independent tests integrate the source density for both entry conventions; they do not compare diffuse entry directly with default PROSPECT. Homogeneous chlorophyll allocation changes its magnitude, not its conditional depth shape. Zero total/selected absorption remains an inactive physical source.

The helper supports Float32/Float64 host preparation; no CUDA or AD execution is claimed for it. Prepared scalar models retain their tested CPU/CUDA contract. The legacy Fresnel expression is checked against angular quadrature for indices 1.25–1.6, covering the native optical database. Its nearly index-matched limit is unsupported. The independent preparation fixture has 140 optical cases and 280 source-depth integrals at 90 digits, including weak absorption and unresolved opaque cases. Normal output tests use relative tolerances 3e-5/2e-12, with subnormal transmission handled by a few-ULP absolute allowance.
