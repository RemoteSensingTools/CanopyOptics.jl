Excitation allocation and several emission sources

A leaf can contain several fluorescence sources, for example photosystem II and photosystem I. They share the leaf's absorbed photons but differ in emission spectrum, photon yield and possibly birth-depth profile. Three small functions compose the existing absorption, escape and spectrum components for any number of named sources; none of them fixes the number of photosystems:

allocated = allocate_excitation(antenna_photons, fractions)   # per excitation bin
ops = emission_operators(probabilities, fates)                # once per source
signal = band_signal(response, ops.front, produced_photons)   # once per source and face

The caller supplies spectra, fractions, yields, birth profiles and detector weights. The core supplies no default for any of them and contains no physiology.

Joint excitation allocation

allocate_excitation(photons, fractions) divides photons in one wavelength bin among named sinks, sink_i = f_i * photons, and returns (sinks = NamedTuple, unallocated = FT) with the keys and order of fractions. photons and the fractions share one floating-point type; mixed precision is a MethodError, as for the other scalar kernels. Valid input has finite photons ≥ 0, finite fractions in $[0,1]$ and

\[\sum_{i=1}^{N} f_i \le 1 + N\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{FT}} ,\]

where $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{FT}}$ is eps(FT). The allowance covers the left-to-right summation of $N$ caller-rounded fractions, so fractions normalized to one in the caller's precision are accepted. Otherwise every sink and the remainder are typed NaN, as in photosystem_excitation.

The remainder is unallocated = max(1 - Σf, 0) * photons. It is never negative and never exceeds photons; within the allowance a sum slightly above one leaves zero. Closure Σ sinks + unallocated = photons holds to about $(N+1)$ rounding errors. When the fractions nearly exhaust the photons, the small remainder has an absolute, not relative, accuracy of that order. With no sinks, valid photons are returned unallocated. A one-sink call reproduces photosystem_excitation(photons, f) exactly, so photosystem_excitation remains the one-sink shorthand.

The scalar call is type stable and allocation free for any number of sinks. It broadcasts over arrays of photons with shared (Ref) or per-bin fractions, on the CPU and on GPU backends.

Two allocation conventions

allocate_excitation does not know what photons are; the caller's fractions state the convention. Published allocations use one of two denominators:

  • fractions $f_i$ of antenna excitation, applied to the output of antenna_excitation;
  • fractions $a_i$ of leaf-absorbed photons, applied to total absorption.

With $h = Q_{\rm ant}/Q_{\rm abs}$ the antenna share of absorbed photons in the same bin, both describe the same sink when

\[a_i = h\,f_i ,\qquad a_i\,Q_{\rm abs} = f_i\,Q_{\rm ant}.\]

Applying $a_i$ after antenna selection counts the transfer loss twice (the sink is too small by $h$). Applying $f_i$ to total absorption omits it (too large by $1/h$). For compatible allocations $\sum_i a_i \le h$, with equality only when the antenna excitation is allocated exhaustively ($\sum_i f_i = 1$).

The remainders differ between the routes:

\[U_{\rm ant} = \Bigl(1-\sum_i f_i\Bigr) Q_{\rm ant},\qquad U_{\rm abs} = \Bigl(1-\sum_i a_i\Bigr) Q_{\rm abs} = (1-h)\,Q_{\rm abs} + U_{\rm ant}.\]

The absorbed-photon remainder also contains the photons that never reached the antenna. Neither remainder by itself specifies nonphotochemical quenching, heat or fluorescence; it is only the part of the input not assigned to a named sink.

To check published $a_i$ against a pigment description, compute $h$ in each bin from the partition and a caller-chosen antenna selection, antenna_excitation(selection, partition) / partition.total_absorbed, and compare it with $\sum_i a_i$ (or with $a_i/f_i$ where both are given). The comparison is meaningful only where the pigment data and the physiological state match the published measurement. In-vitro absorption shares and in-vivo transfer efficiencies are different quantities and should not be mixed in one fraction.

Emission operators, one per source

For excitation bin e and emission bin m, let p[m, e] be the conditional emission probabilities of one source (columns from emission_probabilities, any spectrum and support rule) and F, B, A the front, back and reabsorbed fates of a photon born by that source. Then emission_operators(p, fates) returns

\[M_{F,me}=p_{me}F_{me},\qquad M_{B,me}=p_{me}B_{me},\qquad M_{A,me}=p_{me}A_{me}.\]

fates is either a NamedTuple of three matrices (front = F, back = B, reabsorbed = A), with keys in any order, or a matrix of (front, back, reabsorbed) NamedTuples as produced by broadcasting leaf_emission_fates.(reshape(escape, :, 1), reshape(birth, 1, :)). All arrays share the size and floating-point type of p; mismatches throw on the host. The arrays may be CPU or GPU arrays. Nothing is clipped or renormalized.

emission_closure_residual returns, per excitation column,

\[r_e=\sum_m\bigl(M_{F,me}+M_{B,me}+M_{A,me}-p_{me}\bigr),\]

which is at roundoff level when the fates close (F + B + A = 1): each column of the three operators sums to one for a conditional spectrum and to zero for a dark column. The caller chooses the tolerance.

Dark columns: contract

An excitation bin in which the source has no supported emission on the grid gives an all-zero (dark) column of p; emission_probabilities rejects that case, so only the caller can build such a column. A dark column is permitted only in excitation bins where the source produces no photons. Photons produced in a dark column have no front, back or reabsorbed outcome: they are silently lost, and the closure residual is still zero because it does not see production. For an excitation bin with production and empty emission support, the caller must either expand the emission grid until the support is represented or treat the bin as an error. Nothing is clipped or renormalized to hide the loss.

The caller can detect a violation with existing functions, before applying the operators:

dark = iszero.(vec(sum(p; dims = 1)))   # dark excitation columns of this source
all(iszero, produced[dark]) || error("photons produced in a dark excitation column")

With that check satisfied, the three faces summed over emission and excitation equal the total produced photons of the source to rounding.

Call emission_operators once per source. Sources may differ in probabilities and in fates: a source with a different birth-depth profile (for example because its yield or stoichiometry varies with depth) gets its own fates through the birth-profile argument of leaf_emission_fates; see State-dependent birth profiles. No source container type is introduced; a NamedTuple of operators keyed by source name is sufficient.

Band observation

band_signal(response, operator, produced_photons) evaluates

\[S = \sum_m r_m \sum_e M_{me}\,q_e = r^{\mathsf T} M q ,\]

where produced_photons[e] $= q_e$ are photons already produced by the source in excitation bin e (absorption, allocation and yield applied once, by the caller; zero in dark columns) and response[m] $= r_m$ are dimensionless photon-basis weights on the emission grid. Emission-bin measures are already inside the probabilities and are not applied again. The result is in photons, in the units of produced_photons.

A photon-basis weight is, for example, a detector quantum efficiency (electrons per incident photon) times a filter transmission. A power responsivity in A/W is not a photon weight. If the caller folds the photon energy $hc/\lambda_m$ of the emission wavelength into $r_m$ (for example responsivity times $hc/\lambda_m$), the result is in energy-based units instead of photons; that conversion is the caller's and always uses emission, not excitation, wavelengths.

S is a linear functional of hemispheric face totals. It knows nothing of instrument geometry, collection aperture or fluorometers, and it gives no directional radiance. Dimension or element-type mismatches throw.

Combining several sources

With a yield $\phi_s$ per source $s$ (the caller's value) and the allocated excitation $Q_{s,e}$, the produced photons are $q_{s,e} = \phi_s Q_{s,e}$, and a band observation of the front face is

\[S_F = \sum_s r^{\mathsf T} M_{F,s}\,q_s .\]

Compute one operator per source, multiply the yields in the caller, and sum the band signals. Adding a third source adds one key to the fractions, one operator and one term; no package code changes.

State-dependent birth profiles

Depth coordinate

$z = x/d$ is the normalized geometric depth of a homogeneous plate of thickness $d$, from the front face ($z = 0$) to the back ($z = 1$). It is the same at every wavelength and stays defined when $b = 0$. Because the plate is homogeneous, the effective excitation and emission optical depths from the front face to depth $z$ are $a\,z$ and $b\,z$. Equal layers in $z$ are therefore equal in thickness and in optical depth at every wavelength. Nonuniform pigment changes the local excitation and emission attenuation; that cannot be represented by changing the birth weights alone (see the limits below).

Production: allocation and yield are separate factors

For excitation bin $e$ let $Q_e$ be the antenna excitation of the leaf (photons, for example from antenna_excitation) and $P_{e,k}$ the fraction of it in layer $k$, from birth_layer_fractions applied to the excitation profile of that bin. For source $s$ let $\alpha_{s,e,k}$ be the fraction of the local antenna excitation allocated to the source and $\phi_{s,k}$ its yield: emitted photons per excitation photon allocated to the source. Then the photons produced by source $s$ in layer $k$ are

\[W_{s,e,k} = Q_e\,P_{e,k}\,\alpha_{s,e,k}\,\phi_{s,k}, \qquad q_{s,e} = \sum_k W_{s,e,k},\]

$q_{s,e}$ is the source's production in bin $e$ (the produced_photons of band_signal), and PiecewiseUniformBirthProfile(W[s, e, :]) normalizes the same products into its birth profile. Quenching or the open-centre fraction belong to $\phi$; photosystem stoichiometry and antenna sharing belong to $\alpha$.

An equivalent convention first conditions on the allocation: $Q_{s,e} = Q_e \sum_k P_{e,k}\alpha_{s,e,k}$ is the excitation allocated to the source and $P^{(s)}_{e,k} = P_{e,k}\alpha_{s,e,k} / \sum_j P_{e,j}\alpha_{s,e,j}$ its source-conditioned layer fractions; then $W_{s,e,k} = Q_{s,e}\,P^{(s)}_{e,k}\,\phi_{s,k}$ with the yield alone. With a depth-independent allocation $f_s$, $Q_{s,e} = f_s Q_e$ is the output of allocate_excitation and $P^{(s)} = P$. Use one convention: putting the allocation into $P^{(s)}$ or $Q_{s,e}$ and into the yield counts it twice. A depth-varying allocation makes the source-conditioned excitation profile non-exponential even at constant yield, so such a source needs a layered profile even if its yield does not vary.

If neither allocation nor yield varies with depth, both cancel on normalization: the birth profile is the excitation profile itself, which is exactly what ExponentialBirthProfile describes. With depth-independent allocation and yield the layered fates converge to the exponential ones with second-order error in $1/N$ (observed orders 1.95 to 2.00 from $N = 8$; for $a = 3$, $b = 2$, $t = 0.6$ the largest fate difference is $1.1\times10^{-3}$ at $N = 8$ and $4.3\times10^{-6}$ at $N = 128$). When both allocation and yield are depth-independent, use the exponential profile directly; if either varies with depth, the source needs a layered profile.

With layer values of a smoothly varying allocation or yield (for example at layer midpoints), $W_{s,e,k}$ approximates the layer integral of the production density to second order in $1/N$.

Several illuminations and excitation columns

  • Keep separate excitation columns where possible; each gets its own $P_{e,k}$, production and profile. Keep them separate whenever the conditional emission spectra of the columns differ.
  • If columns must be collapsed, combine production, $W_{s,k} = \sum_e Q_{s,e}\,P^{(s)}_{e,k}\,\phi_{s,e,k}$, and normalize afterwards. Averaging normalized profiles discards the production weights.
  • A yield that depends on the physiological state must be computed by the caller from the combined illumination of all columns first; the state is nonlinear in the light and is not a per-column property.
  • For back illumination, swap the forward and backward excitation amplitudes of the ExponentialBirthProfile ($(r_{\rm exc}e^{-a},\,1)$ instead of $(1,\,r_{\rm exc}e^{-a})$). The layers keep their anatomical order from the front face and the yield field keeps its depth indexing; nothing is mirrored.

Fates and accuracy

The regular-plate fates of a layered profile are closed form. A photon born at depth $z$ starts toward either face with probability $1/2$; its first-flight survival is $e^{-bz}$ to the front and $e^{-b(1-z)}$ to the back, averaged exactly over each layer with $L(x) = -\operatorname{expm1}(-x)/x$, $x = b/N$. The first-flight reabsorption is a sum of nonnegative terms ($-\operatorname{expm1}$ of the boundary depths and $1 - L(x)$, the latter by a series below $x = 1$), so no near-unit escape probabilities are subtracted when $b$ is small; for $b \le \varepsilon_{\mathrm{FT}}$ it is $b/2$, exact to relative $O(b)$ for any profile, which also avoids rounding $x = b/N$ on the subnormal grid. The layer sums, and the normalization of the weights, use compensated (Neumaier) summation, so the relative accuracy does not degrade with $N$. The multiple reflections between the faces use the same closure as the exponential profile. The cost is one exponential per layer.

Accuracy. With $u$ = nextfloat(zero(FT)), the smallest subnormal number, every fate $\hat F$ satisfies

\[|\hat F - F| \le 8(1+b)\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{FT}}\,|F| + (N + 3)\,u ,\]

and the reabsorbed fate has a further $u/(2t)$ when $b$ is below floatmin(FT) and $1 - t \ne 1$ in FT.

  • The relative term includes the rounding of the exponent $b\,j/N$, which is why it grows with $b$.
  • $(N + 3)\,u$ is a derived worst case for underflow. Each of the at most $N$ layer products per face that falls below floatmin is rounded to a multiple of $u$, the face sums are multiplied by factors of at most $1/2$, and the reflection closure adds a few more roundings. It matters only for results below about $(N+3)\,u/\varepsilon_{\mathrm{FT}}$: subnormal results, or results just above floatmin. Results that are not subnormal carry essentially the relative bound.
  • $u/(2t)$ comes from the shared reflection closure: the subnormal product $(1 - t)(1 - e^{-b})$ is divided by about $t$. The exponential profile has the same term. It is below floatmin(FT) and arises only for subnormal $b$.

The tests assert exactly this bound in Float32 and Float64 against an independent closed form evaluated with at least 2048 bits, and with more bits for subnormal $b$. They cover $b$ from $2u$ to 700 (to 800 in Float64), $N$ from 1 to 1000, $t$ from 1 down to $u$, sparse deep sources whose escapes underflow, and the CPU and CUDA batches. A scan of 3510 Float32 and 3330 Float64 cases ($N$ up to 1000, deep, shallow, sparse and dense sources, $b$ from $2u$ to 1500, $t$ from 1 to $3u$) gave a largest ratio of error to this bound of 0.33 and 0.84. Apart from the closure term, no error exceeded the relative term by more than 0.7 $u$, and results that are normal numbers stayed below $4.3(1+b)\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{FT}}$. The underflow term is a worst case and grows with $N$. Closure $F + B + A = 1$ holds within four roundings, and the stored weights sum to one within two, for any tested $N$.

N is a type parameter. The kernel compiles in about 10 ms for any N, runs without allocation and costs about 10 ns per layer on one CPU core. The constructor and birth_layer_fractions unroll over N: their first call compiles in well under a second up to a few hundred layers, about 1 s at $N = 1000$ and 2 to 3 s at $N = 2000$. Tens to a few hundred layers are practical; on a GPU each thread holds the N weights.

using CanopyOptics
FT = Float64
N = 8
depth = ntuple(k -> (k - FT(0.5)) / N, N)             # layer midpoints, 0 = front face

# Caller's layer yields (synthetic): quenched near the front, one dark layer.
# The allocation is depth-independent here, so the weights are P times the yield.
yield = map(z -> FT(0.012) + FT(0.02) * z, depth)
yield = Base.setindex(yield, zero(FT), 6)

escape = RegularPlateEscape(FT(0.8), FT(0.6))          # one emission bin
excitation = [ExponentialBirthProfile(a, one(FT), FT(0.2)) for a in FT[3, 1, 2]]

profiles = map(excitation) do p
    P = birth_layer_fractions(p, Val(N))               # excitation per layer, sums to 1
    w = map(*, P, yield)                               # production per layer
    (total = sum(w), birth = PiecewiseUniformBirthProfile(w))
end
totals = [x.total for x in profiles]                   # photons per allocated excitation photon
fates = leaf_emission_fates.(Ref(escape), [x.birth for x in profiles])

# With depth-independent allocation and yield the layered profile approaches the
# exponential one.
flat = PiecewiseUniformBirthProfile(birth_layer_fractions(excitation[1], Val(64)))
leaf_emission_fates(escape, flat).front - leaf_emission_fates(escape, excitation[1]).front  # ≈ -7.9e-6

All numbers are synthetic. Compared with the depth-independent case, the front-quenched yields lower the front escape of the first excitation bin from 0.274 to 0.265; a yield falling with depth raises it. Multiplying totals by the allocated excitation of each bin gives the produced photons $q_{s,e}$.

What this does and does not represent

  • Equal-width layers with uniform production inside each layer are a discretization of the source, not a model of tissue layers. All layers share the plate's excitation and emission attenuation.
  • Dark layers. A layer with zero yield or zero allocation gets $W = 0$: it produces nothing but still attenuates and reabsorbs emission, because $b$ is a property of the plate. The production $q_{s,e}$ excludes it.
  • Dark sources. If every layer of a source is dark in bin $e$, $q_{s,e} = 0$ and no profile can be formed (the constructor rejects all-zero weights). The caller skips the source in that bin or uses any placeholder profile; with zero production the placeholder does not affect any signal. This is the production side of the dark-column contract above: zero production is what makes a dark emission column admissible.
  • Unequal boundaries (for example a palisade and a spongy layer of different thickness) need another representation; refine $N$ until the boundaries fall on layer edges or use a different profile type.
  • Heterogeneous tissue transport (layers with their own absorption, scattering or interfaces) needs layer optical properties and interface accounting, not birth weights. The two-zone DorsiventralPlateEscape does that for two zones; it has no method for this type, and its DorsiventralBirthProfile zone weights already carry zone-resolved production (zone excitation times zone allocation times zone yield, with depth-independent yield inside each zone).
  • Depth-independent emission spectrum. Production of a source separates as $q_{s,e}(z)\,p_{s,m,e}$: its conditional emission spectrum does not vary with depth. If it does (for example through different photosystem compositions in different tissues), split the source into components, each with its own spectrum, production and profile.
  • The excitation density is the package's effective exponential closure; birth_layer_fractions integrates it exactly but does not make it an angular solution of light inside the leaf.
  • No allocation, yield, rate constant or light response is supplied. If they depend on the local excitation, the caller computes that dependence (for example from $Q_e P_{e,k}$) and any iteration it needs.
  • Reabsorbed photons terminate; they are not re-emitted.

Synthetic worked example

The following block is illustrative: all numbers are synthetic and none is a recommended value. It runs as written.

using CanopyOptics
FT = Float64
emission = FT[670, 690, 710, 730, 750, 770]   # emission bin centres, nm
excitation = FT[450, 550, 650]               # excitation bin centres, nm
absorbed = FT[300, 120, 260]                 # leaf-absorbed photons per bin
coefficients = (chlorophyll = FT(0.8), carotenoids = FT(0.2))

# 1. Antenna excitation per bin, then fractions f of antenna excitation.
selection = CarotenoidAntenna(FT(0.7))
antenna = [antenna_excitation(selection,
               partition_absorption(HomogeneousAbsorption(), q, coefficients))
           for q in absorbed]
f = (psii = FT(0.55), psi = FT(0.4))
allocated = [allocate_excitation(q, f) for q in antenna]
sources = NamedTuple{keys(f)}(keys(f))

# 2. The caller applies its own photon yields.
yields = (psii = FT(0.02), psi = FT(0.004))
produced = map(n -> yields[n] .* [a.sinks[n] for a in allocated], sources)

# 3. One operator per source: its own spectrum and its own birth profile.
spectra = (psii = TabulatedPhotonSpectrum(emission, FT[9, 6, 4, 3, 2, 1]),
           psi  = TabulatedPhotonSpectrum(emission, FT[1, 2, 4, 6, 5, 3]))
escape = [RegularPlateEscape(b, FT(0.6)) for b in FT[2, 1.2, 0.6, 0.3, 0.2, 0.1]]
births = (psii = [ExponentialBirthProfile(a, one(FT), FT(0.2)) for a in FT[3, 1, 2]],
          psi  = [ExponentialBirthProfile(a, one(FT), FT(0.4)) for a in FT[3, 1, 2]])
operators = map(sources) do n
    p = reduce(hcat, [emission_probabilities(spectra[n], StrictStokes(), ex)
                      for ex in excitation])
    fates = leaf_emission_fates.(reshape(escape, :, 1), reshape(births[n], 1, :))
    ops = emission_operators(p, fates)
    @assert maximum(abs, emission_closure_residual(ops, p)) <= 8eps(FT)
    ops
end

# 4. A band with unit weights from 680 to 700 nm; sum the sources.
response = FT.(abs.(emission .- 690) .<= 10)
signals = map((op, q) -> band_signal(response, op.front, q), operators, produced)
front_band_photons = sum(values(signals))

To use fractions of absorbed photons instead, call allocate_excitation(absorbed[e], map(x -> h[e] * x, f)) with h = antenna ./ absorbed from step 1. Passing antenna together with these fractions would count the transfer loss twice.

What is not provided

  • No photon yields, rate constants or quenching states; produced photons are the caller's.
  • No depth-dependent yield. A caller with depth-varying yield builds a PiecewiseUniformBirthProfile per source and excitation bin (regular plate only), as described above.
  • No directional observation, instrument geometry or angular closure; all results are hemispheric face totals.
  • No default emission spectrum, action spectrum, pigment content, antenna transfer fraction or allocation fraction.
  • No conversion to energy; that uses emission wavelengths and is the caller's.
  • No secondary fluorescence. Reabsorption terminates a tracked photon; re-emission of reabsorbed photons would need its own wavelength redistribution and yield accounting and is not part of these operators.

Tests and extension

Tests cover Float32 and Float64; zero, one, three and forty sinks; reordered keys; fraction sums at one, within and beyond the allowance, negative and nonfinite values; invalid photons; closure; no promotion; zero allocations and inferred types; agreement with photosystem_excitation; and agreement of the two allocation conventions after conversion, using the package's partition and antenna selection. Operator tests use two sources with different spectra, support rules (StrictStokes and a finite Stokes band) and fates (regular and dorsiventral plates), dark columns, per-column closure (including a perturbed operator entry), closure against all produced photons, a demonstration of the photon loss when production sits in a dark column, and an independent BigFloat accumulation. Band signals are checked against a hand-computed value, for linearity in each argument, for zero response and against the single-source path composed from existing functions. CPU and CUDA batches cover shared and per-bin fractions, reordered and empty batches, both accepted fate forms, matched permutations of excitation columns and zero excitation columns. A third named source is added in the tests without code changes.

Layered birth profiles are tested in both precisions for closure, inference, zero allocation and isbits; against a 2048-bit closed form for $10^{-12} \le b \le 700$, up to 1000 layers and with dominant-weight blocks, and against Simpson quadrature for a single lit layer among forty; for reduction to the uniform profile (one layer, or equal weights), mirror symmetry under reversed weights, the limits $b = 0$, small $b$ (reabsorption slope $(2-t)/(2t)$), large $b$, $t = 1$ and transmissions down to the smallest subnormal (with $b$ comparable to $t$); against the accuracy bound above for subnormal $b$, subnormal and underflowing results and sparse deep sources up to 1000 layers; for second-order convergence to the exponential profile; for layer fractions against quadrature and the exact integral; for constant, linearly falling and rising yields and a dark layer; for invalid constructors; and in CPU and CUDA batches. An end-to-end test runs two sources with depth-varying allocation and yield, two lit excitation bins and one dark bin through emission_operators and band_signal and compares the band signals with an explicit production-weighted sum over layers built from independently derived spectral probabilities (photon density times bin width) and excitation layer fractions (closed-form integral in BigFloat); it also checks that both production conventions agree and that counting the allocation twice does not.

API reference: allocate_excitation, emission_operators, emission_closure_residual, band_signal, PiecewiseUniformBirthProfile and birth_layer_fractions.
